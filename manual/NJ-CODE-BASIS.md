# ESC Building Manual: New Jersey basis

The manual is written for ESC work in Jersey City and Hudson County, NJ. This sheet records the NJ rules the chapters rely on, how well each is confirmed, and where it came from. Items marked **VERIFY** came from secondary sources or general knowledge and should be confirmed with DCA or the Jersey City Division of Construction Code before being treated as settled.

Research date: November 2026.

## Codes in force (NJ Uniform Construction Code, N.J.A.C. 5:23)

- NJ adopted the **2024 I-codes** (IRC 2024 NJ edition for one- and two-family dwellings, IBC 2024, IMC 2024, IFGC 2024), the **2024 National Standard Plumbing Code (NJ edition)**, the **2023 NEC (NFPA 70)** with NJ amendments, and the **2024 IECC** for low-rise residential. Adopted **August 17, 2026**.
- **Grace period:** a complete permit application with all prior approvals, submitted by **February 17, 2027**, may still be reviewed under the previous editions (2021 I-codes, 2020 NEC, 2021 NSPC). Electrical applications in the window must state which NEC edition the design follows.
- **Plumbing is the NSPC, not the IRC plumbing chapters.** In NJ, IRC Part VII (P-chapters) is replaced by the National Standard Plumbing Code. Don't cite IRC P-sections for NJ work.
- **Existing buildings fall under the Rehabilitation Subcode (N.J.A.C. 5:23-6), not the IEBC.** Work is classified as repair, renovation, alteration, reconstruction, change of use, or addition (defined in 5:23-6.3). A rowhouse gut renovation is usually **reconstruction** (5:23-6.7), which must involve an entire use, primary function space or tenancy. Residential reconstruction provisions (5:23-6.26 and 6.27) were amended in January 2022. New work within the rehab project must meet the subcode's materials and methods requirements (5:23-6.8).
- **Protection of adjoining property: N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.34.** Where protecting a neighbor's property requires entering it and the neighbor refuses, work may not proceed unless a court grants access.

Sources: [ICC NTA: New Jersey adopts new I-codes](https://www.icc-nta.org/code-update/new-jersey-adopts-new-i-codes/), [Jensen Hughes UCC update](https://www.jensenhughes.com/code-alerts/new-jersey-uniform-construction-code-ucc-update), [Cole Schotz](https://www.coleschotz.com/ready-or-not-new-jerseys-construction-code-just-changed/), [NJ 2023 NEC note](https://greensunnj.com/new-jerseys-2023-nec-is-now-in-force-what-it-means-for-your-generator-install/), [DCA Construction Code Communicator, Fall 2026](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/publications/pdf_ccc/Fall_2026_CCC.pdf), [N.J.A.C. 5:23-6](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/codreg/pdf_regs/njac_5_23_6.pdf), [N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.34](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-5-23-2-34).

## Permits, inspections and certificates

- Permits are issued by the **Jersey City Division of Construction Code**. The city lists **prior approvals** that must be in hand before a permit issues, including zoning, Historic Preservation where it applies, and a **JCMUA release for footing and foundation work**. ([Jersey City prior approvals](https://jerseycitynj.gov/cityhall/HousingAndDevelopment/constructioncode/prior_approvals))
- Subcodes are building, plumbing, electrical and fire, each with its own technical section and inspector.
- **Inspections:** requests must be in writing; the inspection is performed within **3 business days**, or another date within 30 days agreed with the enforcing agency (N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.18 as amended 2024; P.L. 2022, c.139 sets the 3-business-day rule). ([DCA Spring 2026 CCC](https://nj.gov/dca/codes/publications/pdf_ccc/Spring_2026_CCC.pdf))
- Completion documents: **Certificate of Occupancy** (new buildings; issued within 10 business days of written application), **Temporary CO**, and **Certificate of Approval**, issued for all work that needs a permit but not a CO, with no separate application (N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.23(m)). An altered building can't be occupied until its Certificate of Approval issues. ([N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.23](https://regulations.justia.com/states/new-jersey/title-5/chapter-23/subchapter-2/section-5-23-2-23))

## Jersey City specifics

- **Historic Preservation Commission:** in a historic district (Van Vorst Park, Hamilton Park, Harsimus Cove, Paulus Hook and others), the owner needs a **Certificate of No Effect (CoNE)** or **Certificate of Appropriateness (CoA)** before work starts, *whether or not a construction permit is required*. Review applies Zoning Ordinance §345-30 (procedures), §345-71 (Historic Design Standards) and the Secretary of the Interior's Standards. A CoA is valid for 1 year (§345-30.B.2). Facade, stoop, window, door, cornice, rear addition, rooftop and deck work is typically reviewed. ([Jersey City HPC](https://www.jerseycitynj.gov/historic))
- **Right-of-way work:** street opening, sidewalk occupancy and dumpster permits come from the **Department of Infrastructure, Division of Engineering, Traffic and Transportation**. Traffic and sidewalk applications should go in at least **2 weeks** before the start date. NYC-style mandatory sidewalk sheds don't apply; pedestrian protection follows IBC Chapter 33 as adopted and the sidewalk occupancy permit's conditions. ([Jersey City Engineering](https://jerseycity.hosted.civiclive.com/cityhall/infrastructure/transportation_resources/engineering_traffic_transportation))
- Utilities in Jersey City: **PSE&G** (electric and gas), **JCMUA** (water and sewer).

## Neighbors and party walls

- NJ has **no party-wall statute** like New York's RPAPL 881 license to enter. Access to a neighbor's property is by written agreement; if refused, work that needs it doesn't proceed without a court order (N.J.A.C. 5:23-2.34).
- **N.J.S.A. 46:10-1:** an owner excavating 8 ft or deeper next to a party wall or adjoining wall must protect that wall at their own expense. **VERIFY** current text. ([N.J.S.A. 46:10-1](https://law.justia.com/codes/new-jersey/2022/title-46/section-46-10-1/))

## Excavation

- **NJ One Call (811):** at least **3 full business days** before digging (weekends and NJ holidays excluded), for everyone including homeowners. Digging must start within **10 business days** of the call. Markouts stay valid for **45 business days**. ([N.J.A.C. 14:2-3.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-jersey/N-J-A-C-14-2-3-1), [811 NJ](https://811beforeyoudig.com/811-states/state-new-jersey/))
- **Frost depth:** the NJ edition of IRC Table R301.2 sets **3'-0" for North Jersey** (Hudson County) and 2'-6" for South Jersey. Confirmed from the NJ amendment text; Jersey City fill and water table often push footings deeper. ([NJAC 5:23-3.21](https://regulations.justia.com/states/new-jersey/title-5/chapter-23/subchapter-3/section-5-23-3-21))
- **Climate zone:** Hudson County is IECC **Zone 4A**. **VERIFY**
- **Radon:** radon-resistant construction is mandatory in Tier 1 municipalities (N.J.A.C. 5:23-10). **VERIFY** Jersey City's tier; a passive sub-slab stub is cheap insurance either way.

## Contractors and trades

- **Home Improvement Contractor registration** (Division of Consumer Affairs): annual registration, HIC number on contracts and vehicles. Contracts over **$500** must be in writing and include the HIC number. The owner may cancel until **midnight of the third business day** after receiving the contract; refunds within 30 days. A 2024 law (P.L. 2024, c.110) moves HICs toward licensing. **VERIFY** current status. ([DCA consumer brief](https://www.njconsumeraffairs.gov/News/Consumer%20Briefs/hiring-home-improvement-contractors.pdf))
- **Licensed trades:** plumbing by an NJ licensed master plumber (N.J.S.A. 45:14C), electrical by an NJ licensed electrical contractor holding a business permit (N.J.S.A. 45:5A; NJ has no low-voltage exemption from the business permit), HVAC by an NJ Master HVACR licensee. A homeowner exemption exists for some owner-occupied work; ESC uses licensed trades regardless.

## Hazardous materials

- **Asbestos:** removal from a residence must be done by a contractor licensed by the **NJ Department of Labor** (N.J.A.C. 12:120, with DOH rules at N.J.A.C. 8:60). The DCA Asbestos Hazard Abatement Subcode (N.J.A.C. 5:23-8) also applies to permits and clearance. ([DCA asbestos guidance](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/alerts/pdfs/asbestos_guidance.pdf))
- **Lead:** EPA RRP applies to pre-1978 work. In NJ, rental one- and two-family and multiple dwellings built before 1978 also need a **lead-safe certificate** under P.L.2021, c.182 (amended by P.L.2024, c.74), inspected at tenant turnover. Relevant when an ESC client rents a unit. Lead abatement (as opposed to renovation) needs NJ-certified abatement firms. ([DCA lead guide](https://www.nj.gov/dca/codes/resources/leadpaint.shtml))
