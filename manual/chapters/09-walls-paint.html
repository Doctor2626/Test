Division 09 · Finishes (Gypsum Board, Plaster, Paint)

Drywall, Plaster & Paint

How ESC picks, hangs, finishes and paints wall and ceiling surfaces in new houses and gutted masonry rowhouses, including fire-rated and sound-isolated assemblies, veneer and traditional plaster, and the repair of historic plaster. Written for lead carpenters, drywall and plaster subs, painters, PMs and owners choosing a finish level.

9.1 Where this work sits in the job

Once board goes up, everything in the cavities is buried, so close-in is the last cheap chance to fix it. Drywall and plaster follow insulation (Chapter 05) and the passed rough-ins for plumbing, HVAC and electrical (Chapters 06, 07 and 08). They come before tile (Chapter 10), and before trim, cabinets and finished flooring (Chapter 11). Paint straddles those trades: primer and first coats before trim and cabinets, final coat and touch-up after. On a typical floor, hanging takes a few days and taping three or four coats over about a week.

Code references are to the 2024 International Residential Code, NJ edition (IRC), and to Gypsum Association publications GA-214 (finish levels) and GA-216 (application). In New Jersey the codes are the NJ Uniform Construction Code (N.J.A.C. 5:23), and the authority having jurisdiction (AHJ) on our Jersey City jobs is the Division of Construction Code. A complete permit application filed by February 17, 2027 may still be reviewed under the 2021 editions, so check which edition the permit set was designed to. The AHJ governs every requirement in this chapter. Rated assemblies must match their tested listing (UL, GA File or manufacturer report) in every detail.

9.2 Before board goes up

The building must be closed, dry, heated and finished behind the walls:

  • Framing, plumbing, mechanical, electrical and insulation inspections passed (Chapters 04 to 08).
  • Building weathertight, windows and flashing complete (Chapter 05). No wet board, ever.
  • Framing at or below 19 percent moisture, and planar: adjacent members within 1/8 in. Rowhouse joists sistered or furred (Chapter 04).
  • Blocking in for cabinets, grab bars, TV mounts, handrails and shower doors.
  • Boxes flush with the finished face, allowing for two layers on rated or sound walls. Nail plates on pipes and cable within 1-1/4 in. of the stud face.
  • Heat on: at least 50 °F for 48 hours before taping and until compound is dry. Dehumidifiers in summer and basements.
  • Every wall and ceiling photographed open, with a tape in frame.

ESC standard

No board goes on until the PM has walked every room with the close-in checklist and the photo record is uploaded.

A 12-ft sheet rarely turns a rowhouse stair. Boom stock in through window openings before sash goes in, or buy 8- and 10-ft lengths for upper floors and accept more butt joints. Stack board flat near bearing walls, never in one pile at mid-span on old joists.

Fire separations and party walls

The IRC townhouse separation provisions (NJ edition) treat attached single-family rowhouses as townhouses. The common wall must be rated 1 hour with an automatic sprinkler system and 2 hours without, running from foundation to roof sheathing. Two-family buildings need a dwelling-unit separation under the IRC two-family provisions. Original party walls of 8 to 12 in. solid brick exceed those ratings on their own, so the job is to keep the masonry intact and firestop every hole we make in it (joist pockets, penetrations, old flues) with listed materials.

On a gut renovation those ratings apply in full to new or replaced separations. Existing party walls and rated assemblies kept in place fall under NJ's Rehabilitation Subcode (N.J.A.C. 5:23-6), and the construction official decides what has to be upgraded. Get that answer during permitting (Chapter 01), and firestop our own penetrations either way.

A frame common wall is built to a listed assembly, commonly two layers of 5/8 in. Type X each side or a shaft-liner system. Other separations:

  • Attached garage (IRC garage separation provisions). At least 1/2 in. gypsum board on the garage side of the house wall, and 5/8 in. Type X on the garage ceiling when habitable rooms are above.
  • Under stairs (IRC under-stair protection provisions). Enclosed, accessible space under a stair gets 1/2 in. gypsum on the walls, the underside of the stair and the soffits.
  • Furnace and boiler rooms. ESC lines them in 5/8 in. Type X.

9.5 Hanging board

  1. Mark framing. Mark stud, joist and blocking locations on the floor and plates so hangers can find them once covered.
  2. Ceilings first. Perpendicular to joists or furring. Walls then support the ceiling edges.
  3. Walls, top row first. Hang horizontally on walls up to about 9 ft, tight to the ceiling board.
  4. Bottom row. Hold the bottom sheet 3/8 to 1/2 in. off the subfloor with a foot lifter.
  5. Stagger butt joints. Ends land on framing. Butts in adjoining rows offset at least 16 in., never on the same stud as another butt on either face.
  6. Wrap the openings. Run sheets across openings and cut them out. Keep joints at least 12 in. from opening corners.
  7. Cut boxes accurately. Overcuts larger than the cover plate get patched with setting compound before taping.
  8. Fasten. Screws set just below the face without breaking the paper, at the spacing in 9.5.1. Work from the middle of the sheet out.
  9. Back-block. On Level 5 walls and ceilings, back-block butt joints that fall between framing so the joint can be finished flat.

Hang vertically on walls over about 10 ft, on steel studs, and where a rated listing requires long edges on framing.

9.6 Ceilings

Ceilings take more low-angle light than any wall. Use the longest sheets the stairs allow.

  • Joists at 16 in. o.c.: 1/2 in. board is allowed; ESC uses 5/8 in. on any ceiling with a skylight, a cove light or a long window wall.
  • Joists or trusses at 24 in. o.c.: 5/8 in. Type X or 1/2 in. sag-resistant board, perpendicular to framing.
  • Old rowhouse joists: often an inch or more out across a room. Sister and plane them (Chapter 04), or fur with shimmed 7/8 in. channel or a suspended drywall grid, which can also decouple for sound.

Common failure

Sagging ceiling board between joists. Causes: standard 1/2 in. board at 24 in. o.c., high humidity, or blown insulation over a ceiling not fully fastened. Use the right board and keep the building conditioned.

9.7 Rowhouse walls: masonry party walls and existing plaster

Each masonry wall in a gut gets one of four treatments, decided per wall in preconstruction (Chapter 01):

  • Exposed brick. Cleaned, repointed with a lime mortar, and sealed or left raw. Wiring runs surface-mounted, in the floor or in a furred chase.
  • Plaster direct to masonry. Traditional brown and finish coats right on the brick, or a modern gypsum or lime basecoat. Thin, durable, and lets the masonry dry inward.
  • Furred board. 1x or 2x furring, or steel studs, held off the brick with at least 1/2 in. air space, then glass-mat board. Leaves a service cavity for wiring.
  • Keep the existing plaster. Covered next.

Common failure

Poly vapor barrier or paper-faced board tight against damp brick. Masonry takes on moisture, and trapped against paper it grows mold. Leave an air space, use glass-mat board, and follow Chapter 05 on exterior masonry.

Existing plaster: restore, overlay or replace

Historic plaster is usually three coats on wood lath, about 7/8 in. thick. It is heavy, hard and quiet. Test before deciding: a hollow knock means delamination, movement under hand pressure means broken keys.

Any work on painted plaster or woodwork in a house built before 1978 falls under the EPA Renovation, Repair and Painting (RRP) Rule once it disturbs more than 6 sq ft per interior room. That means a certified firm and renovator, containment, HEPA dust control and cleaning verification. Test for lead before pricing.

ESC default: On a full gut, plaster is usually removed in Chapter 02 so MEP can run. We restore in place when a room has original cornices or the owner wants to keep it, and we laminate over attached plaster in rooms where only the surface is bad.

  1. Contain and map. Set RRP containment; tape the edges of hollow areas.
  2. Re-attach with plaster washers. Drive 1-1/4 to 1-5/8 in. drywall screws through perforated plaster washers (plaster buttons) into lath or studs, about 6 to 8 in. apart, snugging the plaster back without cracking it.
  3. Re-key with adhesive (for larger areas). Drill 3/16 in. holes through the plaster only, stopping at the lath, about 3 to 4 in. apart. Vacuum, condition, inject acrylic adhesive, and clamp with temporary screws and washers for 24 to 48 hours.
  4. Cut out failed plaster. Square back to sound material; fill with patching plaster, setting compound or shimmed drywall.
  5. Treat cracks. Open the crack slightly, set fiberglass mesh in setting compound, and feather.
  6. Skim and prime. Skim over washers and patches, then prime with a bonding or alkali-resistant primer before finish paint.

New plaster work

Veneer plaster. Blueboard is hung like drywall. Joints get fiberglass mesh, set with the base coat. Base and finish coats go on the same day. Keep the space at 55 °F or warmer, protect blueboard from sunlight, and don't force-dry it with direct heaters. Paint once dry and uniform in color, usually 1 to 2 days.

Three-coat plaster. Over wood lath (restoration), expanded metal lath (curves, repairs), or 3/8 in. gypsum rock lath. Scored scratch coat, then brown coat rodded to grounds at casings and base, then finish over a set, slightly damp brown coat. Gauged lime finish needs 2 to 4 weeks to dry before paint, pure lime longer; confirm with a meter.

9.8 Sound isolation

Sound crosses a wall through the cavity air and through rigid connections. Mass, decoupling, absorption and airtightness each help; the weakest flanking path sets the result. STC is a lab rating; field results run several points lower. The IRC (NJ edition) sets no STC minimum for single-family houses; the IBC requires STC 50 between dwelling units, which matters on a rowhouse converted to two or more units.

ESC default: Mineral wool in all interior partitions and floor cavities around bedrooms and baths. Clips and hat channel with two layers of 5/8 in. Type X for media rooms, ceilings under bedrooms in multi-unit buildings, and frame walls between units. We use damped board where clips won't fit, and we avoid resilient channel because it is too easy to short.

Details that make or break a sound wall:

  • Clips at the manufacturer's tested spacing, commonly 48 in. o.c. along the channel with channel rows 24 in. o.c. Channel ends get a 1/4 in. gap at walls and ceilings.
  • Board on the clip side never touches the floor, ceiling or abutting walls. Leave 1/4 in. and fill with non-hardening acoustic sealant.
  • No back-to-back boxes. Offset boxes on opposite faces by at least one stud bay, wrap them in putty pads, and use box extenders on the clip side.
  • Seal every penetration: pipes, wires, ducts.
  • No ducts or shared ceiling cavities linking the rooms (Chapters 06 and 07).

Inspection hold

ESC inspects every sound assembly after clips, channel, insulation and box treatment are in and before the first sheet goes on. The PM photographs clips, gaps, putty pads and sealed penetrations.

  1. Prefill. Fill gaps over 1/8 in., overcuts at boxes and damaged corners with setting compound.
  2. Tape. Paper tape embedded in taping or setting compound on flat joints and inside corners. Mesh tape only with setting compound. Bed corner bead.
  3. Fill coat. Fill the taper flush, about 6 in. wide. Spot fasteners.
  4. Second coat. About 10 in. wide on tapered joints and 14 to 16 in. on butt joints, feathered. Second coat on fasteners and bead.
  5. Finish coat. 12 to 14 in. on taper joints, wider on butts, with topping or lightweight compound. Third coat on fasteners.
  6. Sand. 120 to 150 grit, or a sanding screen, with HEPA vacuum sanders where the house is occupied or lead is present. Don't scuff the paper.
  7. Skim (Level 5 only). Thin coat of topping or a sprayed high-build surfacer over the entire surface, then light sanding.

Each premixed coat needs to dry fully before the next, about 24 hours at 70 °F and 50 percent relative humidity. Cold or damp air stretches that to two or three days.

  1. Box the paint. Mix all cans of a color together.
  2. Prime. One full coat of the right primer (table above), spot prime patches first.
  3. Inspect primed walls. Raking-light walk (9.11). Fix and spot prime.
  4. Ceilings. Two coats flat.
  5. Cut in. Brush a 2 to 3 in. band at ceilings, corners and trim, just ahead of the roller.
  6. Roll walls. 3/8 in. nap on smooth walls, 1/2 in. on plaster. Keep a wet edge and lay off in one direction.

The standard system is one coat primer and two finish coats. Deep colors may need a tinted primer and a third coat. Latex recoats in about 2 to 4 hours at 77 °F and cures in about 2 to 4 weeks. Most latex paints need surface and air at 50 °F or above; check the label.

9.11 Raking light inspection

We check finish quality under the worst light the room will see, before paint.

Inspection hold

No primer until the PM or lead has done a raking-light walk of every Level 4 and Level 5 surface with the finisher present. A second walk follows primer on Level 5 rooms, stair halls and skylit ceilings. Defects are marked, fixed, spot-primed and re-checked before the first finish coat.

9.13 Inspection holds

Inspection hold

Close-in. Board may not go on until framing, rough plumbing, mechanical and electrical, and insulation inspections are passed and posted, the ESC close-in checklist is signed, and photos are filed.

Inspection hold

Fire-rated assemblies. Some AHJs inspect fastening on rated walls and ceilings (a "screw" or "nailing" inspection) before taping. Confirm at the permit stage. Keep the listing sheet on site, and photograph screw spacing, joint offsets between layers and firestopping before tape covers them.

Inspection hold

Lead. Where RRP applies, the certified renovator performs cleaning verification after plaster and woodwork prep and before painting or reoccupancy. Some jurisdictions and lenders require dust-wipe clearance testing as well.

9.14 Common failures and how to avoid them

Common failure

Cracks at openings, pops and ridges. Seams at head corners, wet lumber, nails, or board not held tight. Wrap openings, check moisture, screw from the center out.

Common failure

Joint flashing and shrink cracks. Joints show through sheen paint, and thick premixed fills crack. Use Level 5 and a quality primer under sheen; prefill deep gaps with setting compound.

Common failure

Shorted sound wall. A screw into a stud, a cabinet bolted to framing, back-to-back boxes or open perimeter gaps. Hold the pre-board inspection and brief every trade who fastens to that wall.

Common failure

Peeling and bleed-through. Latex over unscuffed oil paint peels; stains and tannins bleed through latex. Test with alcohol, degloss and use a bonding primer; spot prime stains with shellac or oil.

9.15 QC checklist

  • Correct board type in every location per 9.3.
  • Rated assemblies match the listing; listing sheet and photos in the job file.
  • Butt joints on framing, staggered, no joints at opening corners.
  • Screw spacing correct, heads dimpled, no broken paper.
  • Boxes cut clean, flush, no overcuts larger than cover plates.
  • Sound walls: clips, channel, putty pads, sealant and offset boxes inspected before board.
  • Corner bead straight and plumb, bullnose caps at three-way corners.
  • Finish level per room achieved; raking-light walk signed before primer.
  • Flatness within targets on a straightedge; inside corners straight.
  • Primer matches the substrate; paint boxed; sheen per schedule.
  • Cut lines straight at ceilings and trim; no roller marks, holidays, drips or flashing in final light.
  • RRP cleaning verification done where it applies.
  • Leftover paint labeled by room, color and sheen for the owner.

9.16 Handoff to the next trades

What the space looks like when we hand it on:

  • To tile (Chapter 10): wet areas left open for the tile sub's backer, board edges straight and 1/4 in. above flanges. Dry tile walls at Level 2 minimum and flat.
  • To millwork (Chapter 11): walls at Level 4 or 5, primed and first-coated. Floors broom-clean and free of compound.
  • To electrical trim (Chapter 08): boxes clean of compound and paint; devices go in after the final wall coat.
  • Back to painting: final coats and touch-up after trim, cabinets and floors.

ESC standard

A finished room is signed off only after a final walk in daylight and at night with the room's own lights on. Flaws visible from normal standing positions get fixed.
